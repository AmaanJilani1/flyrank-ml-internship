Which Pages Deserve a Refresh Review?

A leakage-aware ranking study on anonymized search data · Amaan Jilani · FlyRank ML Internship capstone (Refresh / Content Opportunity Scoring lane)

Abstract

A content team has limited hours, so it needs to know which pages to review first. I asked whether a page’s recent search trajectory can rank pages that later lose more than 20% of their impressions. I built a logistic regression on 18,010 pages from 30 anonymized clients, using only signals from before the label window, and validated it by holding out whole clients. On unseen clients it ranked later decliners better than a persistence baseline (ROC-AUC 0.61 vs 0.57; precision@100 0.85 vs 0.64; base rate 0.62), while a random split overstated performance (AUC up to 0.72) and a deliberate leak reached 0.998. The result is an observed, directional, decision-support ranking with reason codes, not a causal claim.

1. Introduction

The decision: given a limited review budget, which pages should a person look at first? Each page is labeled “declining” if its Search Console impressions in the last 30 days fell more than 20% versus the prior 30 days. The aim is a ranked review queue with plain-language reasons, not an automatic rewrite engine.

2. Data

Release: the anonymized starter slice of the FlyRank ML Internship dataset (30,000 pages, 32 pseudonymized clients; trailing 90-day Google Search Console and GA4 windows). Windows: last 30 days (label); days 31–60 and days 61–90 (features, the latter derived as the 90-day total minus the other two). Excluded: 3,388 pages with no prior-window impressions and 8,602 with fewer than 100 (no visibility to lose), leaving 18,010 eligible pages from 30 clients (the largest holds 32%). All *_last_30d, *_90d, CTR, average-position, tier and trend columns were kept out of the features because they contain the label window. Identifiers were used only for grouping. No client names, domains, URLs or queries appear here.

3. Methodology

Features (25): log impressions, clicks and sessions for both past windows and their change; CTR level and change; keyword volume, competition and CPC; word count (with a missing flag); content age; whether the page was edited more than 30 days ago; content type; search intent. Baselines: (A) “stale 180+ days and visible,” my original rule; (B) persistence, “last period’s drop continues.” Models: logistic regression (shipped, because it is explainable) and gradient boosting (as a check). Validation: 5-fold client holdout, so no client appears in both train and test; a random split to measure client leakage; a shuffle-label test (AUC 0.49, as it should be); and a deliberate-leak test (0.998). A time-aware split was not possible because the data is one snapshot per page.

A signal audit found that staleness is not a general risk signal: pages not updated for 180+ days declined less than average (47% vs 54%), and the oldest content-age quartile declined least (45%). The clearest early-warning signal was a page already losing impressions between days 61–90 and 31–60 (71% decline in the steepest-falling quartile vs 56% in the flat or growing quartiles).

Gradient boosting added nothing over logistic regression, so the simple model is the one used. The edge is real but modest and fades with depth. Scores are over-confident at the top (top decile predicted about 0.88, observed about 0.74), so they are a ranking, not probabilities.

5. Limitations

This is an observed, directional, decision-support result. One snapshot per page means no time-aware validation, so I cannot show the signal holds across periods. Thirty clients, one holding about a third of pages, give variable results (per-client AUC 0.59–0.67). The signal is modest (AUC about 0.61). Impressions can fall for seasonal, technical or demand reasons this data cannot see. About 60% of pages were edited inside the label window, a possible confound. Nothing here shows that refreshing a page changes its traffic, and nothing here describes how Google’s algorithm works.

6. Ranked recommendations

Every eligible page gets an out-of-fold risk score, reason codes (PAST_DROP_CONTINUING, CLICKS_FALLING, HIGH_TRAFFIC_AT_STAKE, VISIBLE_NO_CLICKS, MODEL_RISK_ONLY) and an action tier. Observed decline rate by tier (base 0.62):

Before acting, a person should check the 90-day shape (seasonality, one-off spikes), technical health, remaining keyword demand and recent edits. Never automate rewriting, merging or deleting pages from the score alone. Retrain triggers: base rate moving more than 5 points from 0.62, precision@100 below about 0.70, AUC below about 0.55, a new client with a different content mix, or a change in Search Console reporting.
